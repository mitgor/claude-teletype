Beneath twin suns on desert stone,
A child with restless hands was born—
No herald sang, no banners flown,
Only heat and hunger in the morn.
Yet in the silence of the dunes
The Force moved softly through the sand,
And stars above like silver runes
Foretold the fate of one small hand.

Young Anakin, with brightened eyes,
Would gaze beyond the market’s din,
Past rusted ships and smoky skies,
Toward freedoms locked so far within.
He dreamed in engines, sparks, and speed,
In metal bones and circuits bright,
Believing every noble deed
Could somehow pull the dark to light.

Then came the strangers, cloaked and wise,
Jedi with solemn tones of fate,
Who saw a storm behind the eyes
Of one too gifted, born too late.
“The Chosen One,” the whispers spread,
A balance-bringer yet unknown,
But shadows walked beside that thread
Like wolves that circle flesh and bone.

Across the stars the boy was led,
From desert dust to temple halls,
Where ancient masters bowed their heads
Before tradition’s marble walls.
He learned the saber’s sacred art,
The patient breath, the silent mind—
But fear had rooted in his heart,
And fear is never far behind.

For love had touched him long ago,
A mother’s voice, a hidden ache;
The Jedi warned such ties would grow
Into the chains they sought to break.
But Anakin, with fervent soul,
Could never carve himself apart.
The Order preached serene control;
He carried fire within his heart.

Then came the wars, the drums of flame,
The clone battalions lined for death,
And everywhere they praised his name
With hope upon their every breath.
He flew through chaos like a god,
A comet streaking battle’s seam,
While planets trembled where he trod
And soldiers worshipped the dream.

Beside him walked brave Obi-Wan,
A brother forged through blood and years,
Through shattered moons and battles won,
Through grief concealed beneath their fears.
How many times did laughter rise
Between them in the cockpit glow?
Before the ash consumed the skies,
Before Mustafar’s rivers flowed.

And Padmé—gentle as the dawn,
With kindness rare in halls of power—
Saw not the myth the galaxy spawned,
But simply one who feared the hour.
Their secret love bloomed quietly
Behind the Senate’s gilded veil,
Two hearts entwined impossibly
Against a prophecy grown pale.

Yet dreams became a poisoned tide.
He saw her dying night by night,
Her voice fading, her body wide
With children never kissed by light.
And terror hollowed out his sleep.
What use were all the Jedi ways
If those you loved they could not keep
From slipping into death’s dark maze?

Then Palpatine, with serpent grace,
Drew close beneath political guise.
A kindly smile upon his face,
Galaxies burning in his eyes.
He fed the boy forbidden lore,
Dark promises of death undone:
“The Sith can open secret doors
Closed forever to everyone.”

How easily corruption grows—
Not sudden thunder, but a thread,
A thousand small surrenderings
Inside a frightened, desperate head.
Anakin wanted not to reign,
Nor drape the stars in endless night.
He only wished to conquer pain
And chain mortality itself tight.

But darkness bargains with a lie.
It grants the sword, then takes the hand.
And every fearful compromise
Becomes another harsh command.
Thus in the temple’s sacred halls
The blue blade turned to crimson sin,
And innocence against the walls
Met the machine awakening within.

The Jedi burned. The Republic fell.
The Senate roared approval blind.
And through Coruscant’s iron shell
Rose Empire from the frightened mind.
No longer Anakin by name—
That fragile self was cast away.
Now Darth Vader walked through flame,
A titan clothed in black decay.

On Mustafar the rivers hissed
Like serpents coiling through the night.
Brother faced brother through the mist
Beneath volcanic, bleeding light.
“You were the Chosen One!” the cry
Of Obi-Wan tore through the ash.
The heavens seemed themselves to die
As sabers clashed in molten crash.

Then came the end beside the shore:
A body broken, burning raw,
Limbs severed by the friend he swore
Would stand forever by his side.
And there upon that fiery land,
Half consumed and half alive,
The dark reached out its twisted hand
And taught the ruined man survive.

Encased in iron, fused with pain,
A breathing tomb of endless grief,
Vader arose to serve the reign
That offered neither peace nor relief.
The mask became both cage and face,
The respirator’s haunted cry
An echo through imperial space
Like death itself that could not die.

Across the stars his shadow spread.
Worlds trembled at his cold command.
Rebellion hid in fear and dread
Of the black armor and raised hand.
The Emperor upon the throne
Ruled through terror, fleets, and fire,
But Vader walked the dark alone,
A creature forged from lost desire.

Yet buried deep beneath the scars,
Beyond the rage and mechanized breath,
There lingered faint forgotten stars
Not wholly crushed by years of death.
A memory of desert winds,
Of laughter under open skies,
Of love untouched by wars or sins,
Still flickered dim behind the eyes.

Then came a boy with reckless hope—
A pilot staring toward the sun,
Who carried in his spirit’s scope
The same bright fire as Anakin once had done.
Luke Skywalker, unknowing son,
Walked paths entwined by fate’s design.
And through him, buried and undone,
The old humanity would shine.

The Emperor saw merely prey,
Another soul to bend and break.
But Vader watched in strange dismay
The goodness no fear yet could take.
Again and again the son refused
To strike in hatred, kill in rage.
And something ancient, long abused,
Stirred softly in the darkened cage.

At last beneath the throne room’s storm,
Where lightning split the endless black,
The son stood bruised, but spirit-warm,
While death itself prepared attack.
The Emperor’s fingers spat white pain,
A torrent born of endless hate.
Luke fell screaming to the chain
Of evil tightening with fate.

And Vader watched.

A single breath.

Mechanical. Hollow. Slow.

The years collapsed. The ash. The death.
The child beneath Tatooine’s glow.
The mother lost. The love destroyed.
The brother’s tears. The burning shore.
The emptiness no power filled.
The lie he’d lived forevermore.

Then suddenly the dark lord moved.

Not as servant. Not as slave.

But as a father fierce with love,
At last with something left to save.

He seized the Emperor in rage
And cast him down the endless night,
The tyrant of a fallen age
Consumed within his own dark light.
But lightning scarred the armor deep,
The systems failing one by one,
And Vader, freed at last from sleep,
Collapsed before his dying son.

“Remove my mask,” he softly said.
Not Vader now, but man once more.
And Luke with trembling fingers bared
The face the galaxy abhorred.
There was no monster there beneath—
No demon forged of endless war.
Only a dying man who breathed
And feared the dark no more.

“You were right,” the father said,
Voice thin as smoke in winter air.
“Tell your sister… you were right.”
And silence settled gently there.

So ended Vader’s tragic reign,
Not crowned in triumph, but release.
A soul long mutilated by pain
At last allowed a moment’s peace.
The stars moved on. Empires decayed.
The Force endured through night and fire.
And somewhere in its endless waves
Walked Anakin, redeemed entire.

Remember then the man in black
Not merely as a beast of dread,
But one who lost his chosen track
Through fear of all he loved and fled.
For even in the deepest night,
Where grief and fury twist the soul,
There flickers still a shard of light
No darkness ever swallows whole.
